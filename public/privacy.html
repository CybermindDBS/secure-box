Privacy

Updated 23 November 2026

Secure Box is a vault for passwords, notes and files that encrypts everything in your browser. It's open source (AGPL-3.0): the code this site runs is published on GitHub, so anyone can check what it does.

In short

  • Your passwords, notes and files are encrypted in your browser before they're stored. Only your master password, or your Emergency Kit, can unlock them.
  • They're stored where you choose: your own Google Drive or OneDrive, or a folder on your device. Secure Box has no server of its own that stores them or could read them.
  • There are no Secure Box accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking cookies.

Your vault

  • Everything in your vault (passwords, notes, folders, file names, and each file's own key) is encrypted into one vault file. Each file you store becomes one more encrypted file, under a random name.
  • In Google Drive they're in a “Secure Box” folder. In OneDrive they're in Secure Box's own app folder (“Apps/Secure Box”). On your device they're in a “Secure Box” folder inside a folder you choose.
  • Your master password never leaves your device: your browser uses it to unlock your vault's keys. Nobody else can see it or recover it; only your Emergency Kit can replace it.
  • Encrypt & decrypt works the same way: files and messages are encrypted and decrypted in your browser, and saved where you choose.
  • The demo's sample data stays in your browser tab.

Google and Microsoft

If you keep your vault in Google Drive or OneDrive, you sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own pages and let Secure Box use:

  • Google: your email address, and only the Google Drive files Secure Box creates or that you open with it, nothing else in your Drive (the drive.file permission).
  • Microsoft: your name and email address, and only Secure Box's own app folder in your OneDrive (the Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder permission).

Your browser talks to Google and Microsoft directly. Secure Box keeps the sign-in only in memory while it's unlocked: locking it, or closing it, forgets it. You can take Secure Box's access away at any time in your Google Account or your Microsoft account.

Secure Box's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What your browser keeps

To open the right vault quickly, your browser keeps, on this device only: where your vault was last (Google Drive, OneDrive or this device), the email addresses you signed in with and which of those accounts have a vault, view settings such as grid or list, when your master password last changed, when Secure Box last locked, and which folder a vault on this device is in. None of it is secret: no passwords, keys or sign-in tokens. While you sign in, the tab briefly keeps the one-time codes the sign-in needs. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of it.

This website

The site is a set of files served by Firebase Hosting, a Google service. Like any web server, it sees your IP address and browser details when it sends you those files; Google handles that under its own privacy policy. Secure Box adds no logging, analytics or advertising of its own, and the site's security policy only lets the page connect to this site, Google and Microsoft.

Your data is yours

  • To delete a vault, delete its “Secure Box” folder in Google Drive or OneDrive, or on your device. A vault on this device can also be deleted from Secure Box itself (⋯ → Delete this vault…).
  • There's nothing for us to delete: we never have your data.
